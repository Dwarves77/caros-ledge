# Why Caro's Ledge is being rebuilt

Operator-authored, 2026-10-16, pasted to the coordinator verbatim. Read this before deciding what to build, what to change, or what
"better" means in any slice. BUILD-PLAN-2026-10-12.md says WHAT the new tree contains, PART2-AGENT-PLAN-2026-10-15.md says HOW it
is built, and this says WHY, so every judgement call points the same way the operator's decisions point. Section 7 is the decision
procedure every lane applies; section 4 defines "better"; section 5 is where it stops.

## 1. What the product is

Caro's Ledge is freight sustainability intelligence. Its first customers are freight forwarders who move high-value, time-critical
cargo: art, live events, luxury goods, film and TV, classic and prototype cars, humanitarian cargo. It is built to widen to freight
forwarding in general, and later to other industries, by profile (industry, role, organisation size), never by forking the code.

It has two halves, both core:

- Intelligence: four surfaces, each answering a different question with a different kind of analysis:
  - Regulations: what is binding, when, what it costs, what to do. A compliance-action brief built from a 14-section fact document.
  - Market Intel: what the industry is doing, in numbers and comparisons (deltas, trajectories, lead time).
  - Research: what is emerging, who is studying it, how it changes the planning horizon. Fed automatically, with no editorial queue.
  - Operations: structured regional data for real decisions (cost, labour, materials, infrastructure, feasibility). This is
    content, not a separate decision engine.
- Community: peer information-sharing that breaks the industry's isolation (groups, threads, moderation). It is social only:
  nothing in Community feeds the other pages. It is kept whole and built last.

Across the surfaces: Dashboard (what is new, important and flagged, each item linking to its surface), the Map (a visual filter
over Regulations), Search, and the Assistant. The Assistant is a research helper over platform content; it never synthesises or
decides for the customer.

Underneath is a data machine:

- Sources are registered and rated. Tiers come from an institution class table, never typed by hand.
- Sources are captured in full and minted into items.
- Items are grounded: every fact's quoted text appears word for word in a captured source.
- Items are validated.
- The flywheel connects items and propagates them into derived values that the surfaces read.
- The harness records every run.

Nothing in the machine waits on a human, and customers never upload their own data. The product is external data, made
trustworthy.

The integrity promise is the product. A figure is published with its source's rating; it is never refused because the source is
rated low. What is refused is a figure with no source, or a quote that is not word for word. Gaps are labelled, legal
interpretation is left to counsel, and nothing is invented.

## 2. What went wrong, and why the old code could not be fixed in place

It was far larger than anyone said, and most of it was not product.
- The repository held 1.64 million lines: about 391k of code, 184k of tests, 632k of JSON and 227k of markdown.
- September 2026 alone added about 1.13 million lines.
- The operator had been told it was about 100,000 lines.
- The real product code was about 114k lines. The checking layer around it (gates, rule registries, 37 run-recording families,
  28 workflows, hooks, about 149k lines of tests) was bigger than the product it guarded.

The guards became the failures. Every incident was answered by adding a rule, gate or check, so the system grew a layer per
mistake instead of fixing causes. Those layers then failed on their own: checks that broke CI by themselves; tests that nothing
ever ran, two of them silently failing for weeks; a security guard that passed its "is it there" check while one call could
defeat it; on 2026-10-15, a leftover hook pointing at deleted code that forced a permission prompt on every action for days. A
rule is not a fix, and a check that never runs is not a proof.

Code was changed without being read. One day produced four failed CI runs, all from changes made without reading what they
touched. The "complete" audit had actually read only 2,587 of 5,381 files. Findings were stated as facts while still being
guesses.

About a third of the code was narration, much of it false. Comment histories recounted overruled decisions and old work, and
several contradicted the code beneath them.

The same thing existed many times, and the copies drifted apart: three ways of checking who may call a route; six versions of
the source-tier scale, which disagreed (OECD, IEA, World Bank and IPCC were Tier 2 in code and Tier 3 in the written model);
five copies of the same web-address helper; two "pause collection" switches, one of which failed open; thirteen copies of one
command-line skeleton; five pop-up dialog implementations, none of which kept keyboard focus inside the dialog; 13.5 KB of
styling repeated inside every list row; one 5,048-line file feeding five surfaces.

Features looked shipped and were not. Reading every file found: every Community post is refused, because the composer never
sends the topic it is about; accepting or declining an invitation appears on no page; "promote to public" is listed as shipped
and has no code; settings and the briefing schedule never save; six of seven background runners have nothing that starts them;
the page-collection worker ignores both the pause switch and the kill switch; Market severity is guessed from keywords; 8 of
the 15 fact-document sections never display; no Market figure shows its source rating; Operations has a unit error in its
labour figures; Research has a maturity level that can never be reached.

It relied on paid services the operator had ruled out. Brief writing, search, classification, the Assistant and page fetching
used paid AI and scraping services. The ruling is absolute: everything is free.

The record did not match the system. The 394 database-change files do not reproduce the live database. Production itself is the
only true schema, and it has now been captured as one file.

The process produced the bloat. Rules were written instead of code being read, and work counted as done when CI passed rather
than when the feature worked end to end with everything after it triggered.

The operator's rulings:
- On the cleanup: "I don't want it archived I want it gone." Three cuts took the repository from 1.64 million to 534 thousand
  lines, after the knowledge in the deleted material had been written down.
- "I'm not willing to get rid of features. You need to fix the code. Not remove features."
- "The discipline layer should not be bigger than the code for the actual product."
- "You look at the old code for every single feature, and you write it to be the best possible version of itself by using the
  old code."

## 3. What the rebuild is

The product is being rebuilt from scratch in a new repository, caros-ledge.
- Every feature is kept, each rebuilt from a full read of its old code.
- The database is untouched. Its structure comes in as one file captured from production, and fixes are added as new database
  changes on top.
- Size targets: 15-25k lines of code for the core and 35-60k for the whole platform. The plan budgets about 52k, against about
  155k today, and CI enforces the limits. (Operator 2026-10-16: the platform gate is 75k after wave 2; core stays 25k.)
- One CI workflow. Free services only. No personal details anywhere from the first commit.
- Done means six working demonstrations the operator can open, then Community as the seventh.

## 4. What "better" means

A change is better when it does at least one of these, at a cost smaller than what it returns:
- Capability: more correct, more complete, more trustworthy, or faster for the customer.
- Learning: it improves as the world changes, with no person in the loop.
- Self-maintenance: it stays honest with the least possible machinery.

Anything that does none of these is not built.

Prefer:
- One home for each mechanism. Where copies existed, build one and never bring the others back.
- Rules enforced where the data lives: if the database can hold a rule, hold it there; if code must hold it, hold it at the
  single place that writes the data, with a test that tries to break it; if it is a property of the whole codebase, check it in
  the one workflow. Never build a separate engine that watches everything else.
- Failing loudly and honestly. A failed data load shows an error, never an empty list or a zero. A failed permission check
  refuses. A pause stays paused. Missing setup is reported.
- Fewer requests, layers and files. Thin routes, compact data loading per page, and data fetched once per page.
- Code that explains itself. History lives in git, not in file headers.
- The free path: fixed rules, the class table, search over our own content, plain web requests, or a brief written outside the
  system that the same code then checks.

## 5. Where "better" stops

- Every feature stays. A half-built feature gets finished, not dropped.
- The design stays as it is. Layout, spacing, type, colour and the order of what a page shows match the approved designs and the
  current site, and automated screenshot comparison checks that. Behaviour, data, speed and correctness should improve. If the
  system needs something the design does not show, build to the system and add it to the list of design changes owed; never
  quietly change the look.
- Integrity is not negotiable: word-for-word quotes, rated sources, nothing invented, gaps labelled.
- Standing rules: no human approval step in the data machine; customers never upload their own data; Community never feeds
  other pages; the Assistant answers questions, it does not decide; scheduled page collection stays off while the product is
  being built.
- Examples are not scope. An example describes a whole category; build the category, not the one example.
- Nothing runs alone. Every process triggers the next step and records its run.
- Operator rulings stand until the operator changes them. Improvements that would change the design, how modules connect, or a
  ruling go on the improvements list for discussion, because the operator wants to discuss every improvement. Improvements
  within a piece's budget that change none of those are built and reported.

## 6. Failures not to repeat, and what prevents each

| Failure | What prevents it now |
|---|---|
| Growth by accumulation | CI size limits, an approved list of dependencies, and a header on every file naming the old code it came from |
| A new rule per incident | Fix the cause. Add a check only if it runs in the one workflow and is shown to catch a planted problem |
| Changing code without reading it | Read every affected file in full before writing; the file header names the old lines it was rebuilt from |
| Guesses stated as facts | Every claim is marked confirmed, hypothesis or refuted; numbers come from commands that actually ran |
| Duplicate copies drifting apart | One home per mechanism |
| Half-built features | Done means the feature works end to end, triggers what comes after it, and its demonstration passes |
| Paid services | CI fails if a paid AI library is added; every old paid call has a free replacement written down |
| Silent failures | Visible error states; checks and pauses that refuse rather than allow |
| Records that don't match the system | One database file captured from production; only add-on changes after it |
| Process bigger than the product | One workflow, a handful of checks, tests only on core logic |
| Settings pointing at deleted code | After any deletion, search the repository and ~/.claude for anything still pointing at it |

## 7. How to decide

Answer these in order:
1. Which feature is this, and where is it in its module's list of features? If it is not there, either it was lost (add it) or
   it is not wanted (don't build it).
2. What did the old code do, read in full, including what it got wrong?
3. What is the one home for this mechanism in the new code, and does something already provide it?
4. Does the better version improve capability, learning or self-maintenance at a cost smaller than the gain?
5. Does it keep the design as it is, keep every ruling, stay free, keep integrity, and trigger the next step?
6. Can it be proven by a test that fails first, a check in CI, or a demonstration the operator can open?

If 4 and 5 are yes and it changes neither the design nor how modules connect, build it. Otherwise put it on the improvements list
with your recommendation, and keep building everything else.

## 8. Amendments & Binding Rulings (2026-10-21)

See [OPERATOR-RULINGS-2026-10-21.md](OPERATOR-RULINGS-2026-10-21.md) for the full text of the 2026-10-21 rulings and ADRs:
1. **Typography & Look Governance:** Look and typography changes belong to the operator and the artboards. No waivers or baseline recaptures without an explicit ruling; the red parity diff is the record.
2. **In-Turn Synthesis (`Spec 08`), Verbatim Only:** Stage 8 (`briefs`) synthesis runs inside the turn, emitting only verbatim grounded spans from the primary source or explicitly labelled `[Inference from grounded claim]` lines—zero rule-generated connective prose.
3. **Rulemaking Dates vs. Operator Deadlines:** Regulator rulemaking mandates (`implementing_act_due`, `delegated_act_due`, `guidance_expected`) are pending triggers, never operator compliance deadlines. Pages state only what is known, what is pending, which act it waits on, and when it is due, each from a verbatim span.
4. **Prohibited Overbuilds:** Never build rule-generated `"Act/Invest Today vs. Wait/Stage"` opinions; never build a modal-verb normative fallback that lowers precision; never hardcode physical/financial benchmarks (`CARBON-1`); discard the 5-module `FLY-2` suite (only isolated `Definitions` article duty-holder binding may be tested with before/after yield).
5. **Process & Production Yield Gate:** One worker at a time; one PR and one CI run per slice; every extractor or producer change must report `before -> after` production yield (`no yield number, no merge`).
