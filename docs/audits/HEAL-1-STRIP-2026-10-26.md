# HEAL-1 honest strip, 2026-10-26

Ruling (coordinator, 2026-10-26): the Gate A orphans of instrument items 7a0ead55 (Regulation (EU) 2023/1805, FuelEU Maritime) and 15f63ea9 (Directive (EU) 2023/959, EU ETS) are derived figures and rule-derived dates that no capture states. Under ADR-050 and rule 2 an unprovable sentence does not stay live, so the heal strips it (`stripUnprovable`, plan `scripts/runs/plans/2026-10-26-heal-1.json` revision 2). No Gate-B mint. DIGEST-1 PR B regenerates the figures as DERIVED claims from `statutory_computations` (the `fueleu_intensity_reduction` rows) and the dates as reader-grounded dates.

Reversible: the brief the strip replaces is archived by `ledger_apply_ops` (op `brief`) to `claim_versions` in the same transaction; `healItem` also returns it as `restore.previous`.

## What the strip does (code: `src/features/mint/heal/brief-honest.ts`; revised by HEAL-1c)

- Every standing occurrence of an orphan token is considered, in the sentence or row that carries it. An occurrence inside a longer number (`€15` in `€150`) or a dated day (`March 2026` in `31 March 2026`) is not an occurrence.
- A table row or a list item is one assertion and goes whole, whatever else it states (HEAL-1b).
- A prose sentence goes whole when every figure and date it states is unprovable. A sentence that also states a proven token stays whole (ADR-050: code never cuts a clause out of a sentence) and its unprovable tokens are listed as RESIDUE with the sentence, for the reader (DIGEST-1 PR B).
- Three tokens in one sentence (the Lindner example, €15 / €27 / €44) strip that sentence once and each token counts it.
- The strips are applied whether or not residue remains (an unprovable sentence does not stay live). The item is released only when Gate A then finds no orphan, which is the database criterion 7, not a rule of the strip.

## HEAL-1c: why the HEAL-1b run left both items quarantined (run 40268476812, root run 6f735ab0)

[CONFIRMED] Timeout. Every claim or section write of `ledger_apply_ops` fires `set_provenance_status`, which calls `validate_item_provenance` on the whole item. Measured on production (SELECT, five runs each): 0.42 to 0.48 s per call on 15f63ea9 (85 FACTs, 41 captures, 3.7 MB), 0.19 s on 7a0ead55; the API role's statement timeout is 8 s. 15f63ea9 carries 25 listed attribution violations plus the pass's other ops, so one call needs more than 12 s. The ops are now applied in chunks of 5 (2.4 s on the slowest item), each chunk its own transaction; a failed chunk names its index and how many ops are committed; the next pass re-plans from the database.

[CONFIRMED, two causes] Gate A tokens. Searched the item's captures (production SELECT) for each orphan token under formal variants (decimal comma or point; thousands as space, NBSP, thin space, comma, none; percent with or without a space; EUR or the euro sign; day-month-year as words, dotted or ISO).
(1) A quotation in another convention inside a brief that reads `en`: the captures state `14,5 %`, `91,16 grams of CO 2` and `EUR 1,5` in the brief's own form and FACT claims quoting them exist, but the parser refuses `14,5` under `en` ("not groups of three in locale en"), so the brief figure was never typed and never matched. Gate A now reads such a figure under every locale that can read it and matches it to a claim's FORCED reading (below).
(2) A brief that mixes conventions (15f63ea9: decimal-comma quotations such as `97,5 %` and `78,4 million` beside English thousands such as `1,386,051,745`; vote fr 13, de 13, en 6 of 19, no majority): every separator figure of the brief is refused, `5,000 GT` among them, while a FACT claim minted by the heal states `5 000 GT` (space group) for the same capture text. The two never met. `5,000 GT` is the item's sixth live token (it was not in the stored state, gate 2026-09-04.1, 5 orphans); the heal re-scans with the current gate and found it.
The rule that closes both, digits and separators only (no word is read, nothing is rewritten): a refused brief figure is read under every locale that can read it ("5,000" is 5000 or 5) and is backed when a claim states an EQUAL typed figure (same value, unit, kind) whose own literal FORCES one reading ("5 000 GT", "5 000 GT", "5000 GT" are 5000 under every locale; "14,5 %" is 14.5 under every locale that reads it). A literal ambiguous on both sides ("9,000" against "9,000") still backs nothing, a different digit, unit or kind never backs, and a refusal about the unit set (`€5m`) is never repaired by reading digits.
